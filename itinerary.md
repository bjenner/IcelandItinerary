# Iceland Camper Itinerary

Trip dates: June 13-27  
Vehicle/accommodation: 4x4 camper, likely Toyota Hilux  
Plan style: flexible campground-based route, with highland options only when roads, weather, rental terms, and driver comfort all line up.

## Current Planning Assumptions

- Arrive in Iceland on Saturday, June 13.
- Leave Iceland on Saturday, June 27.
- Stay in organized campgrounds for the entire trip.
- No hotels.
- Treat the camper as both accommodation and transportation.
- Favor flexibility over fixed reservations unless a specific campground or activity requires booking.
- Use the Ring Road as the reliable backbone, then add highland or Westfjords branches depending on conditions.

## Important Constraints

- Campervans should overnight in organized campsites unless landowner permission has been granted.
- Protected areas may require marked/designated campsites.
- Off-road driving is illegal in Iceland. F-roads and marked gravel roads are roads; driving off them is not allowed.
- F-roads often open sometime in June or July, depending on snowmelt and road conditions.
- Check road status before every highland attempt.
- River crossings are at the driver's risk. Rental insurance generally does not cover water damage from river crossings.
- If unsure about a river crossing, turn around.
- Weather can change quickly, especially in the highlands.

## Route Philosophy

The default plan is a counterclockwise Ring Road loop with flexibility for:

- Eastfjords slow travel.
- Mývatn and North Iceland.
- Snæfellsnes or Westfjords, depending on weather and energy.
- A late-trip highland bonus such as Kjölur/F35, Kerlingarfjöll, Hveravellir, or possibly Landmannalaugar if roads are open and conditions are suitable.
- Hot springs and geothermal pools as a recurring trip theme, using the map layer to find nearby options wherever the route lands.

Do not force both the Westfjords and ambitious highland driving if weather or timing gets tight.

## Hot Springs Planning Notes

- The interactive map has a dedicated hot springs / pools layer.
- Treat the layer as a menu, not a checklist.
- Developed baths may require advance booking.
- Small natural hot pots can have changing access rules, local etiquette, fees, crowding, or fragile conditions.
- Do not bathe in unmarked geothermal water. Some springs are scalding, unstable, or unsafe.
- For highland hot springs such as Hveravellir or Landmannalaugar, confirm road openings, weather, and camper rental restrictions before committing.

## Waterfall Planning Notes

- The interactive map has a dedicated waterfalls layer.
- Treat the waterfall layer as a menu of route-aware options, not a completion list.
- Easy route fits: Gullfoss, Seljalandsfoss, Gljúfrabúi, Skógafoss, Kvernufoss, Svartifoss, Dettifoss, Selfoss, Goðafoss, Kirkjufellsfoss, Hraunfossar/Barnafoss, and Dynjandi if the Westfjords branch happens.
- Hike/weather-dependent options: Glymur, Hengifoss/Litlanesfoss, Svartifoss, and longer canyon viewpoints around Jökulsárgljúfur.
- Road-condition or highland-adjacent options: Hrafnabjargafoss, Aldeyjarfoss, Háifoss, and any falls reached by rough interior roads.
- Waterfall days can become wet, slow, and crowded. Carry rain gear, protect cameras/phones, respect closed paths, and avoid getting too close to cliff edges or glacial river channels.

## Volcano Planning Notes

- The interactive map has a dedicated volcanoes / lava fields layer.
- Treat volcano stops as route-aware options, not a checklist.
- Easy fits: Kerið on the Golden Circle, Eldhraun on the South Coast, Hverfjall/Hverfell and Krafla around Mývatn, Grábrók during the west/southwest return, and Saxhóll if the Snæfellsnes branch happens.
- Condition-dependent or highland options: Lakagígar/Laki and Askja. Only consider these if roads are open, weather is stable, daylight and fuel margins are generous, and the camper rental terms allow the route.
- Reykjanes/Fagradalsfjall eruption-area visits require current official access checks. Do not approach closed eruption zones, new lava, gas hazard areas, or emergency work zones.
- Stay on marked trails around geothermal and volcanic ground. Thin crust, unstable lava, gas, steam vents, snow patches, and sudden weather can make an area unsafe even when it looks calm.

## Working Decision Rules

- Check road and weather every evening for the next day.
- Keep fuel above half when heading into remote regions.
- Do not enter highlands late in the day.
- Prefer morning for any possible river crossings, since glacial rivers can rise later in the day.
- Turn around if a road, river, wind forecast, or visibility feels wrong.
- Use campgrounds as the nightly anchor, not hotels.
